01

The biology: heme, electrons, and two ways to jam the machine

Cytochrome P450s are heme-containing monooxygenases — the Phase-I machinery that oxidises most marketed small-molecule drugs. Predicting their inhibition is a core ADMET and regulatory requirement because a drug that blocks a CYP changes the exposure of everything else that CYP clears.

Each catalytic turnover needs electrons delivered from NADPH via cytochrome-P450 reductase to activate molecular oxygen at the heme iron and oxidise the bound substrate. That single dependency is the lever the whole assay design pulls: remove NADPH and the enzyme can bind a compound but cannot metabolise it; add NADPH and turnover proceeds. Everything about the two-arm experiment below follows from this.

Direct (reversible) inhibition

The parent compound binds the enzyme and competes with substrate. No metabolism is required, so it shows up with or without NADPH — the inhibition potency (IC50) is the same in both arms. This is the classic, immediately-reversible liability.

Measured in the −NADPH arm as a clean readout of parent binding.

Time-dependent inhibition (TDI)

A mechanism-based ("suicide") process: the enzyme metabolises the compound into a reactive species that disables it — reversibly, quasi-irreversibly, or by covalent adduction of the heme or apoprotein. Because it needs turnover, it only appears with NADPH, and it drives drug–drug interactions that persist after the parent has cleared.

Detected as a left-shift in IC50 between the two arms.

Why it matters clinically: CYP inhibition slows clearance of co-administered drugs, pushing their plasma levels up — the most common and significant CYP-mediated liability (DDIs), dangerous when the co-medication has a narrow therapeutic index, and uniquely persistent when the inhibition is mechanism-based. Evaluating it is a core FDA expectation, and good in-silico prediction supports the agency's New Approach Methodologies push to cut animal testing.
02

How the data was generated

A two-tier screen — cheap breadth first, expensive precision second — followed by analogue hit-expansion to build a dense, blinded test set. Three isoforms are read by fluorescence; CYP2D6 by mass-spec.

Tier 1 — primary single-concentration screen. Two libraries (Enamine DDS10 diversity set + FDAA FDA-approved compounds) are screened at a single concentration (~49.5 µM), deliberately run in the +NADPH (TDI) condition to catch as many actives as possible. Hits are called with a proper statistical pipeline — variance-stabilising transform, spatial-artifact correction, a per-plate linear model contrasting each compound against the negative control, and Benjamini–Hochberg FDR control — then thresholded on log-fold-change and FDR. A negative log-fold-change means inhibition.

Tier 2 — dose-response confirmation. Each hit gets a 12-point dose-response curve fit with a Bayesian procedure that yields not just a pIC50 point estimate but a 95% credible interval — the honest width of the measurement. Both arms (−NADPH and +NADPH) are collected by default, targeting ~1,500 DRCs per isoform, so a TDI shift can be derived for every compound.

Hit expansion → the test set. The top 25 hits for three isoforms (CYP3A4, CYP1A2, CYP2C9 — 2D6 was still in development) were each expanded into their 10 nearest Tanimoto analogues from Enamine's in-stock catalogue, giving 750 compounds that were then assayed densely against all four isoforms. This is why the training matrix is sparse while the test matrix is dense — and why CYP2D6 is the hard isoform: its test compounds are low-potency analogues selected for other enzymes' hits.

The two readout technologies. CYP3A4/2C9/1A2 use fluorescence: masked "Vivid-style" probes that CYPs metabolise into a fluorescent product, scaled to 1536-well biochemical (recombinant-enzyme, not cell-based) format. CYP2D6's fluorogenic probes gave poor signal, so it was moved to label-free acoustic-ejection mass spec (Echo-MS / AEMS): ~2.5 nL droplets fired into an open-port interface feeding a SCIEX ZenoTOF 7600, tracking depletion of the standard 2D6 substrate dextromethorphan. Same enzyme and NADPH format — but a different noise model, which is one reason 2D6 behaves differently downstream.

And what the same work unlocks beyond the challenge

DDI risk triage

Turn per-isoform inhibition into early co-administration flags, reducing late-stage clinical DDI studies.

MBI liability design-out

Reactive-metabolite structural alerts (alkyne→3A4, furan→2D6) steer chemists away from mechanism-based inactivation.

Cross-CYP selectivity

Map promiscuous vs clean scaffolds across the panel — an organiser research focus too.

Series SAR / matched pairs

The analogue structure of the test set is a ready-made matched-molecular-pair dataset.

Calibrated liability gates

Interval-native predictions give uncertainty-aware thresholds reusable across an ADMET stack.

Structure-informed inhibition

Poses rationalise SAR and connect the regression and structure tracks mechanistically.
